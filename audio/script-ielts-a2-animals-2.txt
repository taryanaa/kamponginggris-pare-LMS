IELTS A2 Listening Test - The World of Animals (Part 2)

[Audio Script - Duration: approximately 2-3 minutes]

Narrator: You will hear a conversation between Lisa and her friend Tom at the zoo. They are talking about wild animals. Listen carefully and answer questions 6-10.

Lisa: Tom, I'm so excited to be at the zoo today! Which animal do you want to see first?

Tom: I really want to see the lions! They are my favorite animals. Did you know that lions are called the "King of the Jungle"?

Lisa: That's interesting! But I thought lions don't actually live in jungles?

Tom: You're right! Lions actually live in grasslands called savannas in Africa. They live in groups called prides.

Lisa: How many lions are in a pride?

Tom: Usually there are about 10 to 15 lions in one pride. The female lions do most of the hunting, while the male lions protect the group.

Lisa: Look! There's the elephant area. Elephants are so huge! How much do they weigh?

Tom: Adult elephants can weigh up to 6,000 kilograms! That's as heavy as four cars together. They are the largest land animals in the world.

Lisa: Wow! And look at their long noses. What do you call that?

Tom: That's called a trunk. Elephants use their trunks like we use our hands. They can pick up things, spray water, and even hug other elephants with their trunks.

Lisa: That's amazing! Oh look, there are some monkeys swinging in the trees. They look so playful!

Tom: Yes, monkeys are very intelligent and social animals. They live in groups and communicate with each other using different sounds and gestures.

Lisa: What do monkeys eat?

Tom: Most monkeys eat fruits, leaves, and nuts. Some monkeys also eat insects and small animals. They spend most of their day looking for food.

Lisa: I can see some black and white animals over there. Are those pandas?

Tom: No, I think those are zebras! Zebras look like horses but with black and white stripes. Each zebra has a unique stripe pattern, just like human fingerprints.

Lisa: Why do zebras have stripes?

Tom: Scientists think the stripes help confuse predators like lions. When zebras run together in a group, their stripes make it hard for predators to focus on just one zebra.

Lisa: That's so clever! Nature is amazing. What about those tall animals with long necks?

Tom: Those are giraffes! They are the tallest animals in the world. A giraffe can be up to 6 meters tall. That's as tall as a two-story house!

Lisa: Why do giraffes have such long necks?

Tom: Their long necks help them reach leaves high up in trees that other animals can't reach. They mainly eat acacia tree leaves.

Lisa: This zoo visit is so educational! I'm learning so much about different animals.

Tom: Me too! It's important to learn about animals so we can help protect them and their habitats.

[End of recording]
