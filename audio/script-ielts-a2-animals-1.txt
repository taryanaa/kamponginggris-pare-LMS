IELTS A2 Listening Test - The World of Animals (Part 1)

[Audio Script - Duration: approximately 2-3 minutes]

Narrator: You will hear a conversation between Emma and her teacher Mr. Johnson about farm animals. Listen carefully and answer questions 1-5.

Emma: Good morning, Mr. Johnson! I'm excited about our visit to the farm today.

Mr. Johnson: Good morning, Emma! Yes, we're going to see many different farm animals. Are you ready to learn about them?

Emma: Yes! I love animals. What animals will we see first?

Mr. Johnson: Well, let's start with the cows. Did you know that cows are very useful animals? They give us milk every day.

Emma: How much milk does one cow give?

Mr. Johnson: A good dairy cow can produce about 25 liters of milk per day. That's enough milk for many families!

Emma: Wow! That's a lot. What about chickens? I see them running around everywhere.

Mr. Johnson: Chickens are wonderful animals too. The female chickens, called hens, lay eggs for us. One hen can lay about 300 eggs per year.

Emma: That's amazing! Do roosters lay eggs too?

Mr. Johnson: No, Emma. Only hens lay eggs. Roosters are the male chickens, and they protect the hens and wake everyone up in the morning with their loud crowing.

Emma: I heard them this morning! What about those big pink animals in the mud?

Mr. Johnson: Those are pigs! Pigs are very intelligent animals. They love to roll in mud to keep cool because they can't sweat like humans do.

Emma: They look so happy in the mud! Are pigs clean animals?

Mr. Johnson: Actually, yes! Pigs are naturally very clean animals. They only roll in mud when it's hot. They never make their sleeping area dirty.

Emma: I see some white animals with thick wool. Are those sheep?

Mr. Johnson: Exactly! Sheep give us wool to make warm clothes. We cut their wool once a year, and it grows back again. It doesn't hurt them at all.

Emma: How often do we cut the sheep's wool?

Mr. Johnson: We shear the sheep once a year, usually in spring when the weather gets warmer. The sheep feel much cooler after their haircut!

Emma: Look at those horses! They're so beautiful and big.

Mr. Johnson: Horses are strong and fast animals. Farmers use horses to help with work on the farm. They can pull heavy things and carry people.

Emma: Can all horses run fast?

Mr. Johnson: Most horses can run quite fast, but some horses are better for working and others are better for racing. The fastest horses can run up to 70 kilometers per hour!

Emma: That's faster than cars in the city! This farm visit is so interesting, Mr. Johnson.

Mr. Johnson: I'm glad you're enjoying it, Emma. Farm animals are very important for our daily life.

[End of recording]
