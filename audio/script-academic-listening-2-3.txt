Academic Listening Script: Island Economy and Sustainability - Part 3

Now, you might wonder what prevents more islands from diversifying their economies. The main barrier to economic diversification in island economies is actually small domestic markets. With limited populations, islands struggle to achieve economies of scale in manufacturing or service industries. This constraint forces them to focus on sectors that can serve international markets, which often means tourism or specialized exports.

Limited access to capital represents another significant challenge. Island nations often have underdeveloped financial markets and limited access to international investment. Banks may be reluctant to lend for innovative projects, and venture capital is often scarce. This makes it difficult for entrepreneurs to start new businesses or for existing companies to expand into new sectors.

The lack of skilled workforce also poses problems. Many islands experience brain drain, where educated young people leave for better opportunities in larger countries. This creates a shortage of the technical and managerial skills needed for economic diversification. Some islands have addressed this through targeted education programs and incentives to retain local talent.

Infrastructure limitations further complicate diversification efforts. Many islands lack reliable electricity, high-speed internet, or modern transportation systems. These deficiencies make it difficult to attract businesses that require sophisticated infrastructure, particularly in technology or manufacturing sectors.
