IELTS A2 Listening Test - The World of Animals (Part 5)

[Audio Script - Duration: approximately 2-3 minutes]

Narrator: You will hear a conversation between David and his sister Kate about insects and small creatures. Listen carefully and answer questions 21-25.

David: Kate, I found some interesting insects in our garden today. Do you want to see them?

Kate: Sure, David! I love learning about insects. What did you find?

David: I saw some beautiful butterflies near the flowers. Did you know that butterflies taste with their feet?

Kate: Really? That's amazing! How long do butterflies live?

David: Most butterflies only live for 2 to 5 weeks, but some species can live up to 8 months. They start as caterpillars before becoming butterflies.

Kate: What about bees? I saw many bees collecting nectar from our roses.

David: Bees are very important insects! They help flowers make seeds by carrying pollen from one flower to another. Without bees, we wouldn't have many fruits and vegetables.

Kate: How many bees live in one hive?

David: A bee colony can have 20,000 to 80,000 bees! The queen bee can live for 2 to 3 years, but worker bees only live for about 6 weeks in summer.

Kate: I also noticed some ants carrying food. They work so hard!

David: Yes! Ants are incredibly strong. They can carry objects that are 50 times heavier than their own body weight. That's like a human carrying a car!

Kate: Wow! Do ants live in families like bees?

David: Yes, ants live in colonies too. Some ant colonies can have millions of ants! They work together to find food and protect their nest.

Kate: What about spiders? I'm a little scared of them, but they're interesting.

David: Spiders are actually very helpful! They eat mosquitoes, flies, and other insects that bother us. Most spiders are harmless to humans.

Kate: How do spiders catch their food?

David: Many spiders make webs from silk that comes from their bodies. The silk is stronger than steel wire of the same thickness! Insects get stuck in the web, and then the spider catches them.

Kate: That's incredible! What about ladybugs? They're so pretty with their red color and black spots.

David: Ladybugs are great for gardens! They eat aphids and other small insects that damage plants. One ladybug can eat up to 5,000 aphids in its lifetime.

Kate: How long do ladybugs live?

David: Ladybugs can live for 1 to 3 years, which is quite long for such small insects. In winter, they sleep under rocks or leaves to stay warm.

Kate: I never knew insects were so fascinating! They're all so different and special.

David: Exactly! Even though they're small, insects play very important roles in nature. We should respect and protect them.

[End of recording]
